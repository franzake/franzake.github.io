My research focuses on developing computational methods and bioinformatic pipelines to characterize post-transcriptional regulation and differential isoform usage at single-cell resolution. I combine advances in sequencing technologies with scalable computational approaches to understand how alternative polyadenylation and isoform regulation shape cellular identity and function.

## Research Areas

### Single-Cell Transcriptomics

I develop computational methods for analyzing single-cell RNA-seq data with a focus on transcript-level resolution. My work addresses challenges in isoform quantification, cell type identification, and characterization of transcriptional heterogeneity in complex tissues.

### Alternative Polyadenylation

Alternative polyadenylation (APA) is a widespread regulatory mechanism that generates transcript isoforms with distinct 3' UTRs. I investigate how APA patterns vary across cell types and biological conditions, and develop computational approaches to quantify and interpret APA regulation from high-throughput sequencing data.

### Isoform Regulation and Differential Usage

Understanding differential isoform usage is critical for elucidating gene regulation beyond expression levels. I design statistical methods and bioinformatic workflows to detect, quantify, and functionally interpret transcript isoform switches across biological contexts.

### Spatial Transcriptomics

Spatial transcriptomics technologies enable mapping of gene expression within tissue architecture. My current work integrates spatial and single-cell data to characterize tissue heterogeneity, cellular neighborhoods, and disease-associated molecular programs in cancer.

### Cancer Genomics

I apply computational genomics approaches to study tumor biology, including fusion transcript detection, tumor heterogeneity analysis, and multi-omics integration. My work aims to identify molecular mechanisms underlying cancer progression and therapeutic resistance.

### Reproducible Bioinformatics Workflows

Reproducibility and scalability are essential for modern computational biology. I develop and maintain Nextflow and nf-core pipelines that enable researchers to perform complex genomic analyses in a standardized, reproducible manner across diverse computational environments.

## Collaborations

I collaborate with experimental biologists, clinicians, and computational researchers to address key questions in cancer biology, developmental biology, and disease mechanisms through integrative analysis of high-dimensional sequencing data.

## Future Directions

My research program will continue to advance computational methods for transcript-level analysis while expanding into multi-modal data integration, spatial multi-omics, and AI-driven approaches for genomic analysis and precision medicine.
